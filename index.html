ME

EDUCATOR, RESEARCHER, AND BUILDER OF SUSTAINABLE SMART SYSTEMS

I am Dr Janaka L. Wijekoon, a Lecturer in ICT (Online Education) at Flinders University in Australia. Over the past fourteen years I have taught, researched, and led across Australia, Japan, Oman, and Sri Lanka, growing from Lecturer to Senior Lecturer, Assistant Professor, and Head of Department along the way, including my PhD and postdoctoral study at Keio University in Japan as a MEXT Scholar. What keeps me going is a simple goal, to excel across all five areas of a successful academic, teaching, research, and institutional, national, and international contributions.

My background is in computer engineering, spanning IoT, network communication, security, and data science, all pointed towards one idea, a Sustainable Smart Society. Lately my focus has been growing towards AI and pedagogy. I am studying how AI can support Authentic Learning, and how Explainable AI (XAI) can strengthen Universal Design for Learning (UDL) so that education works for everyone. I am also drawn to human and wildlife coexistence, and I am building a way to detect and identify elephants at long range, without disturbing them, using seismic signals.

Along the way, student centred teaching has kept my SET scores as high as 4.83 out of 5, which I am proud of. I have helped publish two IEEE Standards, IEEE 1451.1.6 and IEEE 2992, and my applied projects have earned national and international recognition, including a World Champion title at the World Summit Awards 2021, a Silver Award at the Asia Pacific ICT Awards 2020, and the Microsoft Imagine Cup 2024. More recently I secured research grants for Enhancing Authentic Learning Using Generative AI, the Use of Explainable AI in Universal Design for Learning, and Jumbo Net, a non-invasive way to detect elephants at long range.

Leadership has been a big part of my work. I coordinated the Master of IT and Systems program for more than 1,500 students at VIT across four Australian campuses, and I led the Computer Systems Engineering department at SLIIT. As an active IEEE standards contributor, I serve as a Voting Member of P1451.7 and P1451.1.6 and as Vice Chair of P2992. I currently collaborate with Keio University in Japan and SLIIT in Sri Lanka, and closer to home I volunteer on the Governing Council at Rose Park Primary School.

RESEARCH

Most of my work comes back to a few questions I care deeply about. These are the flagship projects behind them, the people I build them with, and where to read the results.

Human and wildlife coexistence
Detecting elephants at long range with seismic signals
In collaboration with SLIIT

This applied research develops a noninvasive, passive way to detect and identify wild elephants at long range using the seismic waves their movement creates in the ground. Instead of cameras or collars, buried geophone sensors pick up footfall vibrations, and our models classify elephant events reliably at distances beyond 160 metres, even in noisy field conditions. Published in IEEE Access in 2024 and 2025, and continued in a saturation-aware detection model accepted for IECON 2026, the work aims to warn communities before elephants arrive, reducing human and elephant conflict and protecting both.

AI in education
Authentic learning and explainable AI for inclusive teaching
In collaboration with SLIIT

This strand explores how artificial intelligence can make higher education more authentic and more inclusive. It studies generative AI as a tool for authentic learning with Generation Z, and Explainable AI (XAI) as a way to strengthen Universal Design for Learning (UDL) so that teaching reaches every student. A further thread treats AI literacy as a foundational skill for an AI-enabled workforce, integrating cognition, competencies, and authentic learning. The work is largely theoretical and pedagogical, published through venues such as the ECIR bibliometric workshop and currently under review, and it feeds directly back into how I design and teach my own courses.

IEEE standards development
Smart transducer interfaces and smart agriculture data standards
In collaboration with Westlab, Keio University

Working with Westlab at Keio University, I help build the international standards that let IoT sensors speak the same language securely. This includes IEEE 1451.1.6, which carries smart transducer messages over MQTT, and IEEE 2992, a recommended practice for expressing and exchanging data in smart agriculture. Alongside the standards themselves, our work addresses security for IEEE P1451.1.6 sensor networks and interoperable data exchange, giving devices a shared and trustworthy way to communicate. As a Voting Member of P1451.7 and P1451.1.6 and Vice Chair of P2992, I contribute to shaping how connected systems are built worldwide.

AI in agriculture
Deep learning and computer vision for early crop diagnosis
In collaboration with the Tea and Coconut Research Institutes of Sri Lanka

This work brings deep learning and computer vision to Sri Lankan agriculture, helping farmers catch problems early. Using image recognition, our systems identify crop diseases, pest infestations, and nutrient deficiencies from photographs, including early diagnosis of Weligama coconut leaf wilt disease and coconut caterpillar damage. The models are packaged into a simple mobile app, so growers can diagnose a plant in the field without specialist equipment and act before losses spread. Developed with the Tea Research Institute and the Coconut Research Institute of Sri Lanka, the aim is practical, accessible technology that puts expert level plant health checks into everyday farmers hands.
